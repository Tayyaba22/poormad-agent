01 — Vision

An intelligence
that belongs to you.

Most AI is rented. You type into someone else's machine, someone else's memory, someone else's rules. PoorMad is the opposite: an agent that lives on your side of the wall — learning, remembering, and improving on your terms.

We are building toward a future where everyone can run an agent that compounds. Not a model you subscribe to, but a mind you own — one that knows your work, protects your context, and gets undeniably better the longer you keep it.

02 — Mission

Ship a real agent.
From a laptop to a fleet.

PoorMad started as one person, one room, one laptop — and a refusal to accept that capable AI had to be closed, centralized, and extractive.

Our mission is to make a genuinely self-improving agent that anyone can run, extend, and trust. Same brain across your terminal, desktop, web, and 20+ messaging platforms. Install it, point it at a model, and it begins to learn. No account required to begin. No lock-in at the end.

03 — Cause

Sovereignty over
your own intelligence.

When the light is blocked, the stars align. The cause behind PoorMad is simple: your agent's memory, skills, and judgment should be yours — not a product someone else can switch off or read.

Privacy by default. Local-first. Open source. Built to be unique and yours only. We don't train on you, we don't hold your context hostage, and we don't sit between you and your own agent. The point of PoorMad is that it remains yours.

local-first

Your machine, your memory

The agent runs where you are. Memory stays in your PoorMad home — nothing leaves your control unless you choose to share it.

open source

MIT licensed

Every line is yours to read, fork, and extend. No proprietary lock, no disappearing feature.

built to be yours only

A distinct identity, not a clone

Own branding, own interface, own learning loop. PoorMad is designed to feel like it was made for one person — because it was.

04 — Research

The learning loop.

PoorMad's core research problem is boring in theory and hard in practice: how does an agent get permanently better from use, without a central model swallowing everyone's data?

Our answer is a tight loop of skills, memory, and orchestration — running locally, improving locally. Here's what we're actually building.

skills

Skills from experience

When the agent solves something non-trivial, it writes a reusable skill capturing the approach — then sharpens it on the next run. Capability compounds instead of resetting.

memory

Recall across sessions

A deepening model of who you are and what you're working on, searchable by meaning. No re-explaining context every time you open it.

orchestration

Tools & subagents

Scheduled jobs, delegation, and a tool registry. Point it at any model and let it coordinate the work.

surfaces

One brain, many fronts

CLI, desktop, web portal, and 20+ messengers share one gateway and one memory — sessions resume wherever you left them.
